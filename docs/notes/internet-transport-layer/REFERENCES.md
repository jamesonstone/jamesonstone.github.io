# Research and claim checks

Working research for GH-79, checked 2026-11-02. These references are editorial support, separate from the draft prose. No traffic statistics or bot prevalence research was performed; the draft makes no numerical population claim.

## Primary historical sources

1. [RFC 793, Transmission Control Protocol (September 1981)](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc793.txt), Introduction §§1.1–1.2 and §2.1.
   - Verified fact: TCP provides communication between processes on interconnected hosts. It supports varied applications, not specifically databases.
   - Editorial consequence: use “transport between databases” only as a metaphor. Do not equate the Internet as a whole with TCP's transport layer.
   - Historical document, not offered as the current TCP implementation specification.

2. [RFC 801, NCP/TCP Transition Plan (November 1981)](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc801.txt), Introduction and Discussion.
   - Verified fact: the transition plan describes interconnecting heterogeneous networks and supporting Telnet, FTP, and mail.
   - Editorial consequence: the foundational architecture was general communication infrastructure. It does not establish an exclusive database purpose or prohibit social uses.

3. [RFC 561, Standardizing Network Mail Headers (September 1973)](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc561.txt).
   - Verified fact: network mail was already in use; this document proposes standardized author, date, and subject fields. Its example is a meeting announcement.
   - Editorial consequence: human communication predates the Web. The argument cannot depend on humans being a late accidental addition.

4. [Tim Berners-Lee, Information Management: A Proposal (March 1989 / May 1990)](https://www.w3.org/History/1989/proposal.html), “Losing Information at CERN,” “Linked information systems,” and “Personal Experience with Hypertext.”
   - Verified fact: the proposal addresses people finding and sharing information across projects through a distributed hypertext system.
   - Editorial consequence: distinguish the Internet's network infrastructure from the Web's information architecture and individual websites' designs. Human discovery is a real original purpose of the Web.

## Status of the argument's other claims

| Claim | Status | Editorial treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sites often represent records and actions held elsewhere | Architectural observation, not universal definition | “Much of” and a concrete illustrative task |
| Delegation can reduce personal navigation | Conditional inference | Depends on actual capability and authorized access |
| Agents will shift investment toward reliability and verifiability | Hypothesis | Present as an implication, not an established market finding |
| Fewer people may visit pages while useful network activity continues | Hypothesis | No population, timeline, or inevitability claim |
| “Dead internet theory is right” | Reinterpretation, not empirical endorsement | Define the alternate meaning and disclaim the inference to bot prevalence or conspiracy |
| Beauty and chosen experience have independent value | Normative position | Preserve explicitly; do not cast all attention as waste |
| Agents maximize utility | Unsupported universal claim | Replace with conditional delegation; include errors, permissions, provenance and obligations |
