# The Internet was Always Just a Transport Layer

Status: unpublished draft for editorial review, 2026-11-02. Initial working title retained. Website publication is outside this change.

## Thesis

Agents make a website's human interface optional for many instrumental tasks. The useful asset is the information or capability behind it, provided it can be accessed reliably and with permission. The stronger distinction is between visiting a place and obtaining a result. Both have value; compulsory navigation loses its justification when it no longer helps the person.

## The correction that strengthens the argument

“Transport between databases” is a productive metaphor for much online commerce and information retrieval. It is neither the technical definition of the Internet nor its exclusive original purpose. TCP connects processes; IP connects networks. Human communication was an early use, and the Web was explicitly built for people to find and share linked information. The argument is about an interface losing its necessity, not history revealing its secret intention.

Dead internet theory is a rhetorical entry point. Recast “dead” as loss of required human attendance for useful work. Do not treat the reinterpretation as proof of bot prevalence, deliberate replacement of people, or coordinated manipulation. None of those claims has been established here.

## Outline

1. Many online tasks require a visit only because humans have been the operators.
2. Separate the underlying network, the Web's information system, and a site's chosen presentation. A storefront is not a technical prerequisite for using a service.
3. Use a concrete comparison: retrieving stock, delivered price, and returns terms across sellers. The user's task crosses sites; each seller designs its own enclosure.
4. Agents can remove some navigation and combine information, shifting competition toward usable capabilities, trustworthy data, and fulfilled commitments.
5. Reframe the dead-internet intuition: useful network activity can continue while fewer people attend individual pages. This is a hypothesis about a possible shift, not measured prevalence.
6. Preserve beauty as a reason to visit. A mural, room, essay, or community can be the experience someone seeks. Exploration and surprise can themselves be useful.
7. Confront the strongest objection: authorization, unreliable agents, transactions, and disputed sources constrain delegation. A new agent interface can also become a gatekeeper.
8. End on the dependency that loses its justification: making people navigate an institution's representation of itself to use what it offers.

## Editorial decisions

- Keep the provocative title for now; acknowledge its metaphorical sense early. Possible more exact alternative: “The Website Was Never the Point.”
- Draft as a proposition with implications, not a product roadmap or universal prediction.
- Avoid claims that agents automatically maximize utility. They act on imperfect representations of objectives and sources.
- Do not reduce human experience to costly friction. Distinguish chosen experience from an imposed route to an unrelated task.
- Keep research and working notes separate from publishable prose.

## Open development questions
