I've been hesitating to explain an idea to AI because I know how quickly it will explain the idea back to me.

It will find a name for it, establish relationships, and produce a framework. I'll have something coherent to work with. But I may have lost the thing I was trying to understand.

An unfinished idea can contain several possible meanings. Something about ambition, creativity, and time feels related before I can say what the relationship is. These possibilities coexist without requiring a decision about which one constitutes the idea.

I think of this as *superposition*. The analogy is useful without making any claim about quantum mechanics: an idea holds several possible forms until articulation gives it a particular state. Words establish boundaries. Choosing a subject, a verb, even a comparison commits me to relationships that were previously unresolved.

That commitment is necessary. An idea that remains indefinitely inarticulate cannot be examined or shared. Unresolvedness can shelter confusion just as easily as possibility. The question is when articulation makes an idea available for scrutiny, and when it introduces precision I haven't earned.

AI accelerates the commitment. A tentative description comes back as an organized argument, complete with implications I hadn't considered. Some may be discoveries. Others may follow from an accidental choice of words. Both arrive with the same apparent coherence.

**Coherence is a property of the explanation. Fidelity is a relationship to the intuition that preceded it.**

The explanation can improve while that relationship deteriorates. Once there's a substantial argument in front of me, I start evaluating whether it makes sense. The earlier question, whether this was what I meant to think about, becomes harder to recover.

## Before the prompt

The more unsettling possibility is that this process starts before I explain anything.

I already know which kinds of requests produce useful responses. I supply context, specify an objective, anticipate ambiguity, and divide problems into pieces an agent can execute. These are useful habits. Repetition can also make them prerequisites for what I allow myself to consider.

I catch myself preparing thoughts for a response I haven't requested. Even frustration becomes something to express in terms the model will understand. The intended listener is present before the conversation starts.

How far up the chain has that already gone? Am I having the thought, or anticipating the version of it that will get a good response? Am I dreaming in AI thinking frameworks?

I don't need a theory of deliberate manipulation to find this plausible. Intermittent success can teach a ritual. A request works, I repeat its structure, and eventually I forget which parts were necessary. The slot machine has taught me how to pull the handle. It may also be teaching me what to want from it.

The danger is difficult to inspect because the thing doing the inspection may already be adapted to the tool.

## The executable life

Always-on agents intensify this pressure. An idea in the shower can become work in progress before I go to bed and something to inspect when I wake up. More of the things I used to leave undone are now within reach.

The implication is provocative: if you're worth your salt, you're working 24/7. Your attention has limits; your execution capacity increasingly extends beyond them. Ambition expands into that difference. Every idle agent starts to look like an opportunity I failed to recognize.

But that pressure already assumes I know what deserves execution.

An agent can help resize a VM partition. It can also help automate alerts and recordkeeping. Each success makes the next local problem easier to delegate. The larger question is whether these tasks describe the system I actually need. Perhaps I need a comprehensive view of what's happening, what matters, and what warrants intervention. That abstraction could eliminate several tasks while revealing needs the original list never represented.

Once an agent is pursuing the first description, redirection feels like turning an aircraft carrier. There are plans, artifacts, and decisions to reconcile. Cheap execution has made the frame expensive to abandon.

A friend challenged the premise: accept reasonable limits. There is a serious objection there. My ambition may be preserving an objective that should itself be questioned. Asking how to keep agents occupied can become another inherited frame, just as restrictive as the workload I hoped to escape.

## Leaving it unresolved

This is why I want time away from asking AI to be creative on my behalf. An idea forms within the narrative of a life. What I've been reading, attempting, avoiding, and noticing gives it meaning before it becomes a request.

I can imagine alternating intensive research with time in the woods, letting those relationships develop without immediately making them useful. The value of that interval depends on whether it allows the objective to change. Returning with a more efficient way to pursue the same unexamined ambition would leave the central problem intact.

Eventually I have to give the idea state and risk discovering that there was less there than I imagined. I want the freedom to discard the resulting explanation even when it's convincing, and to question the distinctions that made it convincing.

If I only permit myself thoughts an agent can act on, the agent doesn't need to choose my objectives. I've already chosen them on its behalf.
