title: "Superposition I: Articulation"

An idea exists in superposition until articulation assigns it state. Several possible abstractions coexist before language distinguishes their boundaries and relationships. Explaining the idea selects one configuration. The resulting precision can conceal that selection: a description appears to reveal what the idea was, although it has helped determine what the idea becomes.

Choosing a noun establishes a category. Choosing a verb assigns agency or causation. An analogy imports relationships that may never have belonged to the intuition. These commitments make an idea examinable, but they also determine which objections become intelligible. A formulation can exclude an alternative before either has been considered explicitly.

AI rapidly elaborates the provisional interpretation. An incidental noun becomes an organizing principle; an analogy acquires the authority of a mechanism. The argument can become increasingly coherent while losing fidelity to the possibility that prompted it. Internal consistency establishes relationships within the explanation. It cannot establish that this was the explanation worth developing.

The danger is difficult to detect because coherence rewards continued refinement. A contradiction looks like something to repair inside the framework. Yet it might be evidence that the framework's boundaries were mistaken. Improving the explanation and recovering the original question can demand opposite actions: one adds distinctions that protect the argument; the other discards the distinction around which it was built.

Preserving superposition means treating articulation as an experiment. A second formulation should be allowed to contradict the first, rather than becoming an amendment to it. The useful comparison concerns what each formulation makes visible, what it renders irrelevant, and which assumptions both quietly preserve. An intuition deserves scrutiny, not immunity; unresolvedness alone is no evidence of depth. But neither should an articulate explanation acquire ownership of the thought merely because it arrived first.
