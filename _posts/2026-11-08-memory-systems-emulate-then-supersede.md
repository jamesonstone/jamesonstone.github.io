Designing the system means emulating the right parts of human memory and taking the advantages machines actually have.

It does not mean assuming human methods and structures are correct. They might not be. We emulate first because what exists now is a file cabinet with an API, and the human pattern is already better than similarity search: reasoned bricks, a toward, a stage that does not show what it will not use, incubation that does not write truth.

Those methods are a starting analog, not a destination. The lake is what makes superseding possible. Keep the traces. Replace the judgment layer when a better one appears. Let evidence decide whether the new algorithm is an improvement.

"Better" cannot mean denser recall. It would have to mean a truer toward, cleaner serve, and bricks that have reasons. Superseding without that standard just produces a more powerful cabinet. New categories may appear that we do not yet have good names for. The hierarchy — experience, memory, lesson — is where we start, not where we are required to end.
