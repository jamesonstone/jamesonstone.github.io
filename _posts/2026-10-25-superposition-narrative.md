An idea does not arrive as an isolated proposition. It emerges amid unfinished work, accumulated disappointments, ambitions, and a story about what a life is becoming. That context helps determine why a particular connection becomes interesting. Extracting the sentence can preserve its apparent content while severing the relationship that made it worth thinking about.

Kahneman's [System 1/System 2 account](https://kahneman.scholar.princeton.edu/document/8) distinguishes automatic, associative impressions from effortful monitoring and reasoning. Context and expectations influence what comes readily to mind; deliberation can inspect an impression or accept it. This does not map neatly onto superposition and articulation. Intuition can suppress ambiguity, while deliberate thought can reopen incompatible interpretations. Slowness is no guarantee of correction.

The proposed extension concerns the narrative surrounding an intuition. An idea about eliminating unnecessary work might express an insight into an organization. It might also protect an identity built around being indispensable. The same proposition can serve both functions. Establishing its validity would not establish that pursuing it serves the life in which it arose.

Three questions therefore need separate answers: why did the idea occur, what warrants believing it, and why does it matter? Personal significance neither validates nor refutes a claim. But removing that significance does not produce a complete account of what deserves attention. Kahneman and Riis separately distinguish [lived experience from its remembered evaluation](https://kahneman.scholar.princeton.edu/document/7); that distinction concerns another dimension of judgment, not another name for System 1 and System 2.

An agent can investigate a proposition while missing the ambition it expresses. It can also elaborate that ambition so convincingly that its original purpose escapes examination. The philosophical task is selective decoupling: assess the claim without using biography as evidence, then restore enough context to ask whether the claim addresses the need that produced it. Abstraction should permit movement between those levels. An idea stripped of its context can be logically defensible and existentially beside the point.
